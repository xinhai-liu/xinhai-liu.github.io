Professional biography

From machine learning to credit infrastructure

I am a researcher, policy adviser and educator working across artificial intelligence, credit reporting, financial technology and the data economy. My research focuses on the information infrastructure behind modern finance: how data is generated and shared, how institutions assess credit and risk, and how intelligent systems can improve financial decision-making while preserving accountability and consumer protection.

I was trained as an engineer and data scientist. I received my PhD in Electronic Engineering from KU Leuven, where my research examined multi-view data, clustering, text mining and large-scale information analysis. That technical foundation later became the basis for a broader research agenda in credit scoring, financial networks, big-data risk analysis and AI-enabled financial services.

From 2011 to 2018, I worked at the Credit Reference Center of the People’s Bank of China, while also undertaking postdoctoral research at the PBOC Research Institute. My work during this period covered credit-reporting infrastructure, big-data analytics, credit-risk management and the development of China’s financial information system. I later served as a visiting scholar at the London School of Economics and held a part-time research role at Peking University’s intelligent-finance research center.

Today, my work increasingly connects three domains that are often studied separately: credit infrastructure, artificial intelligence and the personal data economy. Current interests include AI for credit and post-loan management, enterprise-network and systemic-risk analysis, MyData and data portability, consumer credit and credit rebuilding, and cross-border credit services for firms expanding internationally.

I am particularly interested in comparative and cross-border research. China, Europe, Korea and the United States have developed different institutional answers to the same underlying questions: who can use financial data, how creditworthiness should be assessed, how consumers should be protected, and how innovation can be introduced into highly regulated markets. Much of my current work is therefore designed to connect academic research, policy dialogue and real-world financial practice.
